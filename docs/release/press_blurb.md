## Pitch (one paragraph, ~80 words)

Dungeon Scramble is a turn-based roguelike where every turn is a small puzzle. Roll sixteen dice into a 4x4 tray, then trace a single path through them: each die you step on fires its face, from Hit and Block to Combo, Boost and Freeze. Monsters always show their next move, so every turn is a fair fight you can plan for. Pick from four origins and four classes, collect 56 loot dice and 27 relics, and descend through three floors to the Big Bosses.

## Short version (3 sentences)

Dungeon Scramble is a roguelike dungeon crawler where you roll a 4x4 tray of dice and trace one path through it to attack, block and heal. Monsters telegraph their next move, so each turn is a puzzle you can solve rather than a coin flip. Designed by Jeremy and Katlynn Friedman, it began as their own board game. Coming to iPhone and Android, with Floor 1 free and no ads.

## The story (~150 words)

Dungeon Scramble began as a tabletop game designed by Jeremy Friedman and his wife, Katlynn Friedman. Jeremy has a master's in engineering and worked as a mechanical engineer before switching careers toward teaching math. Together they built the game and its mechanics: a word-game-style grid of dice where, instead of spelling words, you trace a path from die to die and every face you land on is an attack, a block or a trick. Add monsters, loot and a dungeon to kick your way through, and the board game was born.

Then Jeremy started building a digital version to test it, and it kept growing. The computer could handle the bigger numbers, stacking effects and bosses with whole move lists that would bog down a game played by hand. Somewhere along the way he decided, in his words, "this is a video game now." The board game edition was set aside, and the video game took over.
