# How comparable small dice/roguelike indie games found their first players

Research date: 2026-10-28. Method note: in this session most article pages could not be fetched directly (network allowlist blocked gamedeveloper.com, windowscentral.com, vice.com, localthunk.com, wikipedia.org, *.itch.io devlogs, reddit.com). Findings below come from search-engine extracts of those pages. Where an extract quotes a developer, the quote is attributed to the underlying page, but it was not re-read in full. Treat exact figures as "reported by the source named", and re-check them before quoting publicly.

## Dicey Dungeons (Terry Cavanagh): the 7DRL jam origin and the free alpha period

### Takeaway
The 7DRL jam build itself got almost no attention (3 ratings). The audience came from about a year of **free, public, browser and itch alphas released every couple of weeks**, which also served as the feedback loop that shaped the design. Cavanagh already had a large following from VVVVVV and Super Hexagon, so his discovery story is not representative of an unknown developer. His iteration method transfers well, though.

### Cited Findings
- The first version was a prototype for the Seven Day Roguelike (7DRL) jam in March 2018. Cavanagh, in his own words: "You can actually play the earliest version of the game in a web-browser" (Wireframe interview, circa 2019). [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.org/features/dicey-dungeons-terry-cavanagh-interview)
- The jam entry "Dicey Dungeons #7drl build" placed #4 for Fun and #51 overall, from only **3 ratings**. The jam itself did not spread it. [itch.io 7DRL 2018 rating page](https://itch.io/jam/7drl-challenge-2018/rate/234586)
- Cavanagh's own words: "Once I thought it might be going somewhere, I started releasing free alphas every couple of weeks to get feedback. Around the … eleventh alpha or so, I started bringing other people in" (artist Marlowe Dobbe, composer Chipzel). [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.org/features/dicey-dungeons-terry-cavanagh-interview)
- The early builds changed the design directly. Players could get stuck on turn one after a bad roll, "so I added a 'prepared spell' slot." [Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.org/features/dicey-dungeons-terry-cavanagh-interview)
- The game was announced around May 2018, after about 3 months of development, with a free in-progress version available. Alpha devlogs ran on itch, including "Dicey Dungeons v0.11", with the last pre-launch alpha v0.17.2 on 2019-05-30. The full PC release was 2019-08-13; consoles and mobile came later. [itch devlog](https://terrycavanagh.itch.io/dicey-dungeons/devlog); [v0.11 devlog](https://terrycavanagh.itch.io/dicey-dungeons/devlog/43755/dicey-dungeons-v011); [Wikipedia (via search extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicey_Dungeons). A Grokipedia page claims weekly builds from v0.7 on 2018-05-18 (low-reliability source, unverified).
- The alphas used placeholder MIDI pop tunes, and players grew attached to the later placeholder music. This is a sign the free alphas built a real community before launch (2021 interview). [Nintendo Life, 2021](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/01/feature_dicey_dungeons_terry_cavanagh_and_chipzel_on_inspirations_characters_and_the_crystal_maze)
- Dicey Dungeons later went to the IGF (Road to the IGF interview). That page could not be fetched. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-cavanagh-houston-dobbe-s-i-dicey-dungeons-i-)

### Inferences
- For Dungeon Scramble, the transferable part is the **cadence**: a free playable build plus short numbered devlog posts every 1-2 weeks, each naming what changed because of player feedback. That gives returning players a reason to come back and gives itch's devlog feed something to surface.
- Jam ratings are not a distribution channel by themselves. 3 ratings is typical for a jam entry.

### Gaps
- No download or play counts were found for the free alphas. No source says where Cavanagh first posted the alphas beyond his own itch page and Twitter. His existing fame is a confound.

## Slice & Dice (tann): early itch/browser versions and the following before Steam/mobile

### Takeaway
Slice & Dice was sold on **itch.io and Google Play from mid-2021**, years before the 2024 Steam and iOS launch. Steam says it took about 7 years. The developer worked on itch and Android first and kept a steady devlog. No written interview about how its first players were found turned up.

### Cited Findings
- Full game released on itch.io 2021-06-16 and on Google Play 2021-07-26. [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Slice_&_Dice)
- Devlog versions: 1.0.2 and 1.0.3 (July 2021), 2.0 (2022-10-02, "an enormous amount of new content"), a "still working" post on 2023-06-29 ("I never speculate about release dates"), then 3.0 on 2024-03-20 ("came out today on iOS/Steam/Android and here"), and later 3.x patches through 3.2.13 (2025-05-21). [itch devlog](https://tann.itch.io/slice-dice/devlog); [2.0 post](https://tann.itch.io/slice-dice/devlog/434748/slice-dice-20-release); [still working](https://tann.itch.io/slice-dice/devlog/552747/still-working); [3.0 post](https://tann.itch.io/slice-dice/devlog/701202/30-release)
- The Steam page credits tann with "~7 years" of work and lists a demo. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1775490/Slice__Dice/)
- The itch comment pages, hundreds of comments long, show long-running beta players comparing difficulty across versions, e.g. "in 1.0.3 I won on easy most of the time, in the beta I win more like 20%". The game had an engaged tester community on itch before Steam. [itch comments](https://tann.itch.io/slice-dice/comments?before=72)
- tann answered questions on *The Spelunky Showlike* podcast ("Into the Depths: Slice & Dice, Part 3"). No transcript was available. [Chartable](https://chartable.com/podcasts/the-spelunky-showlike/episodes/176198537-into-the-depths-slice-dice-part-3)

### Inferences
- Slice & Dice is the closest model to Dungeon Scramble's plan: a portrait-friendly dice game, an itch and Android audience first, and a free demo with a paid unlock. Its itch comment section and beta branch worked as the community hub.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found on how its first itch players arrived, its early numbers, or whether a free browser version existed. A Reddit or r/roguelikes history could not be checked because reddit.com is blocked here.

## Luck be a Landlord (TrampolineTales / Dan DiIorio): itch demo, streamers, Early Access

### Takeaway
A solo developer with a short free demo (about 20 minutes) and a long Steam Early Access (Jan 2021 to Jan 2023). The breakout came from **streamers picking it up**, not from the developer's own marketing. At 1.0, most visibility came from Steam's New & Trending, which the developer credits to the Early Access history.

### Cited Findings
- Steam Early Access launched 2021-01-08 and 1.0 on 2023-01-06. The game is also sold on itch. [Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1404850/view/3951311355465859078); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1404850/Luck_be_a_Landlord/)
- By March 2021 it was described as "something of a surprise hit," with 1,000+ user reviews (Very Positive), and a demo that "only takes 20 or so minutes." [GamingOnLinux, 2021](https://gamingonlinux.com/2021/03/luck-be-a-landlord-gives-slot-machines-a-little-deck-building-flavour)
- A separate demo app exists on Steam. [Steam demo](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1439770/Luck_be_a_Landlord_DEMO/)
- Wikipedia (via search extract) says DiIorio struggled financially, and "this changed when the game suddenly became popular among streamers." [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luck_Be_a_Landlord)
- Northernlion's 2023 stream of Luck be a Landlord was LocalThunk's initial inspiration for Balatro. Streamer play spreads a game's influence as well as its sales. [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/balatro-localthunk-luck-be-a-landlord-northernlion-inspiration/)
- Developer's own launch post (Jan 2023): exact sales are not disclosed, but launch-week copies were "roughly 30 times the amount sold in the first week of October 2022." More than 75% of launch-week Steam impressions came from **New & Trending**, where the game sat for over a week. It averaged about 150 concurrent players about 3 months before 1.0 and never fell below about 100 after endless mode was added. He credits Early Access with earning that placement. [TrampolineTales blog, 2023](https://blog.trampolinetales.com/whats-next-for-luck-be-a-landlord/)
- Third-party estimate: about 807k units and about $4.8M gross (tracker estimate, not official). [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1404850/Luck_be_a_Landlord/sales-revenue)
- A YouTube interview with TrampolineTales exists but was not reviewed. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6UpGWUoufk)

### Inferences
- A short, complete demo (about 20 min) that ends on a hook suited streamers: one sitting, legible on screen, and big moments when combos fire. Dungeon Scramble's "every die fires its face" chain gives the same kind of readable payoff on stream.

### Gaps
- No primary account of the first itch players, or of which streamer started the 2021 wave. Commonly repeated claims about specific streamers were not verified here.

## Balatro (LocalThunk): demo, Next Fest, Reddit/Discord, streamers

### Takeaway
LocalThunk's own Reddit and Twitter posts produced almost nothing (48 wishlists at end of May 2023, 183 by June 10). Growth began when **one mid-size streamer (Dan Gheesling) played it**, who then pitched it to **Northernlion**. After that came repeated demos and two Next Fests. For a tiny free demo, the transferable lessons are: get the game in front of a few streamers who fit its genre, and don't cap the demo so hard that streamers finish it in one sitting.

### Cited Findings (primary source: LocalThunk's "The Balatro Timeline" blog post, 2025, via search extracts and secondary coverage)
- Work began in December 2021, when he decided to "burn three weeks of holiday time." [LocalThunk blog](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh); [PC Gamer on the timeline](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/the-official-balatro-timeline-documents-the-history-of-2024s-biggest-game-as-its-developer-went-from-obsessed-with-making-it-to-shocked-at-the-reception)
- Wishlists: **48 at end of May 2023** (one secondary source says 43), **183 by June 10**, **2,440 by end of June** after Dan Gheesling's stream ("a massive streamer for my game (and a great guy!)"), **28,661 by end of July** after Northernlion, **77,380 by end of October**, and **208,401 at launch** (Feb 2024). [LocalThunk blog](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh); [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/prince_t_research/the-road-to-7-million-how-balatro-actually-reached-the-world-18mb); [opgamemarketing](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/do-great-games-actually-sell-themselves)
- The demo, created in June 2023, was capped at **50 rounds**. Northernlion finished it quickly and was given a full beta key, and the team then realised the demo needed updating. LocalThunk later called the cap a gate he didn't need. [LocalThunk blog via search extract](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh)
- GamesRadar quotes him: after quitting his job, "five months and a few posts on Reddit later, people just started playing it." Elsewhere he says he "didn't do any of the marketing things that he should have." [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/exploring-balatros-hype-its-ingenious-twists-on-poker-and-its-mysterious-creator); [PocketGamer.biz, 2025](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/i-dont-think-i-would-have-rated-balatro-higher-than-an-8-and-i-made-the-damn-thing/)
- A new demo arrived in late September 2023, with the release date announced. At February 2024 Next Fest the demo was reported as the **8th most-played demo** of the fest. [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/balatro-creator-recommends-trying-2-special-games-this-steam-next-fest-and-both-roguelikes-are-on-par-with-the-poker-themed-hit); [Gardiner Bryant](https://gardinerbryant.com/pay-attention-to-the-steam-next-fest/)
- Launch analysis (secondary) lists the pillars as multiple demos over time, multiple Next Fests, and an active Discord. [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250207-balatro-successful-launch-strategy)
- Mobile port and demo-feedback interview, 2024. [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2024/03/18/balatro-interview-mobile-port-localthunk-dlc-plans-updates-new-jokers-demo-feedback/)

### Inferences
- Even a game that sold 5M+ had fewer than 200 wishlists after its first posts. A slow start from self-posting is normal and is not a verdict on the game.
- Streamer reach is a **chain**: a mid-size streamer who loves the genre passes the game on to a bigger one. Pitching a handful of small to mid streamers who play dice and deckbuilder roguelikes beats a broad blast.
- Dungeon Scramble's Floor 1 demo should be long enough, and replayable enough (classes, depth), that a streamer doesn't exhaust it in 20 minutes. A wall that a streamer hits quickly cost Balatro goodwill.

### Gaps
- Next Fest wishlist deltas for Balatro specifically were not found. Discord size over time was not found.

## Other dice/roguelike games with documented itch or browser origins

### Takeaway
The pattern repeats: **a jam or free itch build → slow start → streamers or itch staff pick it up → a commercial store**. Several developers report that itch alone gave little traffic or discussion. The free build mainly served as proof and as a funnel.

### Cited Findings
- **Die in the Dungeon** (dice-bag roguelike): began as a Brackeys Game Jam entry (Feb 2021) on itch and was developed in spare time from 2021 to 2023. A free prologue, *Origins*, came Dec 2023, Steam Early Access Feb 2025 (publishers HypeTrain Digital / Sidekick), and 1.0 on 2026-05-01. The jam version is still free as "CLASSIC." [Wikipedia (extract)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_in_the_Dungeon); [itch devlog](https://alarts.itch.io/die-in-the-dungeon/devlog/675071/die-in-the-dungeon-origins-now-on-itchio)
  - Chris Zukowski (2025) calls it a best case: "streamers and the itch.io team found it, loved it, and pushed it." He reports 1,694 reviews and a 2,848 concurrent-player peak at EA launch. [How To Market A Game, 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/22/more-games-that-made-the-itch-io-to-steam-transition/)
- **Vampire Survivors**: first itch build 2021-03-31, about 9 months before Steam. Luca Galante hoped "to get 100 people to play the game on Itch.io" and **did not reach that**. He moved to Steam partly because there was no player discussion on itch. Steam went from 14 concurrent players (Jan 5, 2022) to 1,143 the next day after streamers and YouTubers picked it up. Afterwards the itch web build was kept "pretty much like a demo." [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/how-vampire-survivors-went-from-hobby-project-to-game-of-the-year/1100-6511980/); [How To Market A Game, 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/); [Vice (extract)](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-vampire-survivors-went-from-obscurity-to-27000-people-playing-at-once)
- **Loop Hero**: a Ludum Dare 45 entry (Oct 2019, theme "Start with nothing") that was "not playable" at the deadline. The team kept going and released a public alpha demo ("LooPatHerO") on itch in Dec 2019, Devolver published, and the full release came March 2021. [itch alpha](https://fourquarters.itch.io/loopathero-demo); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/loop-hero-interview); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/closing-the-loop-four-quarters-on-the-making-of-loop-hero/)
- **Backpack Hero**: a free HTML5 browser game on itch, then a Steam demo "similar to the version on Itch" (devlog 2022-06-27), then Early Access in Aug 2022. [itch page](https://thejaspel.itch.io/backpack-hero); [devlog](https://thejaspel.itch.io/backpack-hero/devlog/397807/backpack-hero-has-a-demo-on-steam)
- **Peglin**: the alpha demo opened to itch users while development continued (2020). A demo arrived around May-June 2020 (dates differ: wiki says 2020-05-20, SteamDB says 2020-06-16), with Steam EA on 2022-04-25. A gif contest for keys ran 2022-01-14. Peglin later went to mobile. [itch devlog](https://gedig.itch.io/peglin/devlog/145839/welcome-to-peglin); [release plan devlog](https://gedig.itch.io/peglin/devlog/194054/peglin-release-plan-demo-available-now); [Peglin wiki](https://peglin.wiki.gg/wiki/Peglin_(Game)); [TouchArcade mobile](https://toucharcade.com/2024/01/01/peglin-mobile-update-miniboss-mines-redoublet-relic-iphone-android)
- **Card Crawl** (Arnold Rauers / Tinytouchtales, mobile-first): Feb 2015 iOS release, praised and well ranked "for at least a little while," which expanded the studio's profile in mobile communities. It reached Google Play "with little fanfare" as a free game with a €2.99 unlock, the same model Dungeon Scramble plans. A 2016 one-year retrospective exists (wnhub, Chinese), but its discovery details were not retrieved. [TouchArcade 2017](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/23/classic-reload-card-crawl/); [Xataka Android](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/card-crawl-una-divertida-y-adictiva-mezcla-de-dragones-y-mazmorras-con-hearthstone/amp); [Game Data Podcast with Rauers](https://www.gamedatapodcast.com/p/arnold-rauers-tinytouchtales-geo-gods-card-crawl-miracle-merchant-data-programming); [PocketGamer.biz spotlight](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indie-spotlight-tinytouchtales/)

### Inferences
- Itch on its own rarely produces momentum. In these cases it worked as a **free proof of fun** that streamers, itch staff and later publishers could find. The jump came from someone else's audience.
- Two of these games (Die in the Dungeon, Peglin) and Slice & Dice reached mobile after a PC or itch community existed. None of the cases found began mobile-first from a web demo, so Dungeon Scramble's path is less trodden.

### Gaps
- Dicefolk, Dice & Fold, Dice Throne digital, Roll for the Galaxy, Dice Gambit, Dicey Elementalist, Card Thief, Pocket Rogues: no documented early-traction stories were found within the search budget. Wordle (a free web game that spread through share-grids) was not researched in detail.
- No developer "what I'd do differently" quotes were found, apart from LocalThunk's comments on his 50-round demo gate and on doing little marketing.

## Demo conversion data: what share of visitors play, how long, what makes demos sticky

### Takeaway
On itch, **browser games are played by about 37% of page viewers versus about 7% for download-only games** (median, lifetime). That strongly favours Dungeon Scramble's browser demo. Steam Next Fest demos convert a median of about 16% of demo players to wishlists. No reliable public data on session length was found, so the game's own telemetry is the best source.

### Cited Findings
- How To Market A Game itch benchmark (2025, developer-reported lifetime stats): median browser game about **1,582 views → 590 browser plays (~37%)**. Download-only median about 6.9%. At the 70th percentile, about 13,000 views. [How To Market A Game, 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/)
- Itch forum anecdotes vary widely (e.g. 1,391 views and 0 browser plays, or more plays than views in a day). Developers suspect itch counts a browser play only after some playtime, not on every reload (unverified). [itch forum thread](https://itch.io/t/2033799/can-you-share-your-stats-and-analytics-here)
- Steam Next Fest (Zukowski surveys, as reported second-hand): median demo-player → wishlist conversion **16.33%** (30th percentile 12%, 70th percentile 20%) in Feb 2026. Another wave reports 19.3% (June 2026). Demos launched well before the fest reportedly earned about 2.5× the wishlists of demos launched during it. These are secondary blog summaries and self-reported samples skewed toward developers who market heavily. [steampageanalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-next-fest-results); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-to-build-steam-wishlist)
- Zukowski's Vampire Survivors post suggests a demo made by taking the full game and adding a short timer, and timing development to hit one Next Fest before launch. [How To Market A Game, 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- Stickiness examples from the cases above: a short complete demo (Luck be a Landlord, about 20 min); demos refreshed repeatedly (Balatro); a too-short cap backfired with a streamer (Balatro 50 rounds); frequent free updates with visible feedback-driven changes (Dicey Dungeons alphas).

### Inferences
- With a browser demo, the main lever is **page views**, because about a third of viewers will try it. Work on getting the itch page in front of people (screenshots or GIF of a die chain firing, devlogs, the tags "dice", "roguelike" and "mobile", itch jams, and streamer outreach).
- Use the existing telemetry (new-player funnel) to measure what public data can't: first-fight completion and Floor 1 completion rates.

### Gaps
- No public data was found on median session length for itch browser demos, on mobile-browser vs desktop play share on itch, or on itch-demo → Google Play conversion. No mobile-specific (Play Store) pre-registration conversion data was researched.
